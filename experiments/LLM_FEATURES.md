# LLM-proposed physical features

Six agents, each with one physics lens (longitudinal powertrain, transmission signature, braking / regeneration / coasting, lateral inertia and geometry, car-following interaction, speed-dependent capability), proposed window features with a physical rationale and numpy code while seeing only the channel definitions and a training-only sample (2,727 windows that are in the training split of both schemes). Six verifier agents ran the code on that sample, fixed bugs, measured class separation and dropped degenerate or duplicate features; one auditor screened the merged list for recording-artefact shortcuts. Everything below was fixed before any evaluation split was touched. The features are then fed to the same classifiers and protocols as the plain-statistics baseline.

Proposed: 74; registered after verification, audit and self-test: 34.

### Auditor's summary

Audit 1: Reviewed 12 features against train_sample.npz (2,727 windows; 107 pseudo-models formed from distinct footprint x powertrain); all reported AUROC/Spearman values reproduce exactly. Verdicts: 5 keep_with_caveat (brake_free_decel_share, gas_pressed_decel_frac, gas_pressed_decel_share, no_brake_decel_p10, curv_gain_midspeed, drive_specific_power_p95 - six including the weak specific-power one), 6 exclude. The regen/one-pedal family is physically genuine: every ICE pseudo-model brakes its hard decelerations (flag off in 0-5% of samples below -1.5 m/s^2) while BEV models are 24-95% unbraked, AUROC survives removing the dominant one-pedal BEV model (0.83 -> 0.79) and every BEV model separates individually. But three of the five brake-related features are one feature (no_brake_decel_share is numerically identical to brake_free_decel_share; the energy share is 0.99 rank-correlated), so two are excluded as duplicates; the ICE high-share tail is highway coast-down at the -0.3 m/s^2 threshold (a -0.6 threshold removes it and raises AUROC to 0.91); two ICE models have an unreliable brake flag; and all pedal features measure the driver-selected regen mode as well as the drivetrain, with a composition-driven footprint correlation that must not be read as size physics. lat_acc_gain_highspeed is excluded because its code leaves the v^2 factor in place and the feature is a speed measurement within class (Spearman 0.53-0.88 with mean speed; between-car spread smaller than within-car spread); the speed-normalised curvature version performs better. yaw_response_lag_s is excluded as a signal-pipeline artefact (per-model medians 0.09-0.26 s for similar footprints, extreme lags on cars whose steer-rate channel has 4 distinct values, class-dependent missingness, class differences of a third of a sample). Both lead-response gains are excluded because their discrimination comes from automation-controlled samples (AUROC 0.55-0.58 human-only vs 0.67 automation-dominated; DAS-only gain bimodal across BEV models by controller type) and the human-only remainder is driver habit at accelerations far below any vehicle's capability. curv_gain_midspeed is the only solid size feature (rho_fp -0.48, between-model spread > within-model) but should be conditioned on human-steered samples: its small BEV/ICE signal lives entirely in the DAS-steered subset (0.71 vs 0.51). Main gaps: a low-speed Ackermann gain / two-term understeer fit (rho_fp -0.67 on this sample), launch torque build-up, gear-shift dips, one-pedal stop/creep behaviour, coast-down a(v) shape, acceleration-envelope shape, and steering-effort shape. Audit code and per-window values:  (audit_feats.py, analyze.py, analyze2.py, analyze3.py, feat_values.npz).

Audit 2: Reviewed 29 re-admitted (A) and 12 gap-filler (B) features on physics, shortcut risk and code-vs-rationale, re-running all code on the 2,727 training windows (all stated statistics reproduce; scripts and the feature matrix are in ). Verdicts: keep 6 (curv_gain_lowspeed, yaw_gain_speed_elasticity, steer_geom_gain, ackermann_gain_2_6, ackermann_gain_6_12, steer_effort_saturation), keep_with_caveat 22 (mostly regen/one-pedal/creep/hold settings, driver habit, EPS tuning or port-specific pedal-flag and low-speed aEgo behaviour), exclude 13. Exclusions: (1) duplicates - stop_without_brake_frac and brakeless_stop_fraction are identical to each other and 98% identical to stop_nobrake_share (retained); lowspeed_nopedal_accel is a 0.97 near-duplicate of creep_accel_mps2 (retained); coast_decel_highspeed is a 0.93 subset of liftoff_decel_moving with no added signal. (2) automation leaking in - decel_onset_ttc's size signal exists only in automated-following windows (rho 0.275 vs -0.002 in human following); lead_accel_response_lag is dominated by ACC/openpilot response lag in 57% of its windows and shifts with the automation share. (3) rationale not implemented - accel_envelope_ratio_15_25 and sustained_accel_cv actually measure the one-pedal gas-flag duty cycle (rho -0.52 / +0.86 with cruise-under-gas share; pedal-agnostic or truncated versions lose the signal); coast_c2_aero and steer_understeer_term are unidentifiable per 60-s window (wrong sign in 60% / 44% of windows, scatter several times the physical range); steady_follow_thw is pure driver choice with its size direction contradicted identically within ICE and BEV; gas_to_accel_latency_s shows a non-monotone 0.30/0.75/0.60 s within-ICE size pattern that mass cannot produce while the dead-zone-free moving tip-in latency has no size signal, pointing to platform-level sensor/hold/transmission behaviour. (4) recording artefact - yaw_hf_gain_ratio tracks steering excitation (rho 0.43) and reads the yaw-estimator noise floor/filtering. Notable caveats for kept features: steer_effort_slope gets part of its separation through the drive-level torque normaliser (shape-only AUROC 0.37 vs 0.31); lead_decel_to_ego_decel_delay should be re-issued with das == 0 over the event; the B steering-gain features could drop the das condition to raise coverage since the transfer gain does not depend on who steers. Redundant-but-distinct estimators (curv_gain_lowspeed / ackermann_gain_2_6 / steer_geom_gain; liftoff_decel_moving / coast_decel_low_speed_mps2 / coast_c0_retard / liftoff_decel_mps2) were kept for the classifier to weigh.

Physical cues no feature covers: Low-speed Ackermann gain (2-6 m/s, kappa/delta ~ 1/(i_s L)): near-pure geometry; on this sample rho_fp -0.67 with BEV/ICE AUROC 0.49 (coverage 0.21). A two-term fit delta = c0 + (i_s L) kappa + (i_s K_us/g) kappa v^2 over all moving human-steered samples separates wheelbase x ratio (rho_fp +0.43) from the understeer coefficient (BEV < ICE, AUROC 0.42) and removes the speed confound of both proposed lateral gains.; Torque build-up / launch response: delay and jerk from a human gas_pressed onset to the acceleration plateau (instant electric torque vs throttle, turbo and torque-converter lag) - a powertrain cue no feature covers.; Gear-shift signature: torque-interruption dips in a_mps2 during sustained human acceleration at speed-dependent spacing, and downshift steps while coasting - present only with step-ratio transmissions, absent in BEVs and eCVT hybrids.; Stop and creep behaviour: share of stops (standstill entered) reached with brake_pressed = 0 (one-pedal hold), deceleration in the last 1 m/s before standstill, and creep speed / latency after brake release without gas (ICE automatic creep vs configurable or absent BEV creep).; Coast-down shape a(v) with both pedals released: the v^2 coefficient (C_d A / m) and intercept (rolling resistance plus engine braking) as a mass/drag cue, rather than only a share; for BEVs it must be restricted to regen-free lift-offs, otherwise it re-measures the regen setting.; Acceleration-envelope shape: ratio of the upper-quantile acceleration at ~25 m/s to that at ~10 m/s (P/(m v) decay for BEV vs gear plateaus for ICE) - less driver-dependent than a single p95 of v*a.; Steering effort: steer_torque_norm is unused; the normalised torque vs lateral-acceleration relation (EPS assist curve, linearity, saturation) is a vehicle property whose shape survives the per-drive normalisation.; Track width has no observable in these channels except through the understeer coefficient, so size classification rests on wheelbase x steering ratio plus composition confounds; the pedal features carry a composition-driven +0.17 to +0.23 footprint correlation, so the shortcut audit should report size performance within each powertrain class..
